Product brief

Conductor

A household's own AI assistant — runs on hardware the family already owns, answers wherever a message was sent, and reaches for outside help only when a question genuinely calls for it.

Reachable via Telegram · iMessage · phone call Runs on a Mac at home Household data Google Calendar & Tasks

Most household coordination — the calendar, the grocery list, who's picking up whom — doesn't need a data center. Conductor keeps that work on a computer already sitting in the house, answers questions in whichever app a family member already has open, and treats the wider internet as something to consult on purpose, not by default.

Capabilities

Calendar, kept honest

Check, add, move, or cancel events; find a free slot; get reminded before something's due — proactively, without having to ask.

remind me at 4 to call Grandma

Scheduling for more than one person

Name who else needs to be there and Conductor checks their real availability too, then sends them an actual calendar invite — not just a note on the household calendar.

find 30 minutes this week that works for me and Jamie

Shared lists

Groceries and chores, added and checked from any channel, backed by the same Google Tasks lists everyone can already open on their phone.

add oat milk and paper towels

Reach it however's easiest

Text it on Telegram, iMessage it, or call and talk to it — including interrupting mid-sentence and having it actually stop and listen.

a phone call, with barge-in

Knows when to ask for help

A short, fixed list of harder tasks — real research, planning the coming week, summarizing the last one — hand off to a more capable model with live web access. Everything else answers instantly from the machine in the house.

can you research the history of Mount Rushmore

Runs code, safely

Can write and execute small scripts on request, inside a sandbox with no view of the rest of the computer — nothing it runs can touch a family photo or a document.

a quick calculation or reformatting job

In practice

Telegram

Standing in the cereal aisle: "add oat milk and paper towels to the list."

Shows up on the Groceries list immediately — visible to whoever does the shopping next, in the Google Tasks app they already use.

Phone call

Driving: "what's on the calendar tomorrow afternoon?" — then, cutting in mid-answer: "actually, remind me to call Grandma at 4."

Barge-in stops the reply immediately and registers the new request — no need to wait it out or repeat anything.

iMessage

"Find 30 minutes this week that works for me and Jamie."

Checks both calendars, proposes a real opening, and — once confirmed — sends Jamie an actual invite on their own calendar, not just a line on the household one.

Any channel

"Can you help me plan out the upcoming week?"

One of the fixed tasks that always escalates — the reply is grounded in the real calendar and grocery list, not generic advice, and comes back from the more capable model automatically.

How it works

One assistant, two tiers of thinking.

flowchart LR
    FAMILY["Family members"] -->|"Telegram, iMessage, or a phone call"| CONDUCTOR["Conductor\nruns at home, sandboxed"]
    CONDUCTOR --> LOCAL["Local model\nhandles routine requests"]
    CONDUCTOR -.->|"a fixed task, or an explicit ask to research"| CLOUD["Cloud model\nharder reasoning, live search"]
    CONDUCTOR <--> GOOGLE["Household calendar & lists\nsame data, every channel"]

The local model is the default for everything — it never leaves the house and there's no per-question cost. A short, deliberately chosen set of tasks always escalate to a more capable cloud model instead of guessing at "how hard" a question is; the household's own calendar and lists live in Google, so the same state shows up whether it was set by voice, text, or directly in the Calendar app.

Guiding principles

  • Local by default

    Routine conversation runs entirely on hardware the household owns. Nothing leaves the house unless a specific task requires it.

  • Escalate on purpose

    The assistant never decides for itself that a question is "too hard." A fixed list of tasks, or an explicit ask, are the only paths to the cloud.

  • One shared truth

    Calendar and lists live in the apps the family already has open — no separate database only the assistant can read.

  • Only the access a task needs

    Checking whether someone's free doesn't require seeing what they're doing. Adding them to an event is a normal calendar invite, not a door into their calendar.

  • A person decides who's family

    Who the assistant recognizes — and what it can act on their behalf — is set by a person editing a file, never something a conversation can talk it into.

  • Sandboxed by construction

    It can run code on request. It was never given a way to see the rest of the computer while doing it.
